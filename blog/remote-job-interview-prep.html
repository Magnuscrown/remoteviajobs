How to Prepare for a Remote Job Interview

By Magnus Crown • 6 min read • September 2026

Remote job interviews are different from in-person interviews. There's no handshake, no office tour, and no reading body language as easily. Here's how to prepare and stand out.

1. Test Your Tech Setup (Day Before)

Nothing ruins an interview faster than bad audio or a frozen screen. Test everything the day before:

  • Internet: Use wired ethernet if possible, or sit near the router
  • Camera: Test with Zoom or Google Meet. Position at eye level
  • Microphone: Use headphones with a built-in mic, or a USB mic
  • Background: Clean wall, tidy room. Avoid beds and messy areas
  • Lighting: Face a window or use a ring light. Never sit with light behind you
  • Backup: Have your phone ready with mobile data in case Wi-Fi fails

2. Choose the Right Environment

Find a quiet room with a door. Tell family or roommates not to disturb you. Put pets in another room. Silence notifications on your phone and laptop.

3. Dress Professionally (Even From Home)

Wear what you'd wear to an in-person interview. Solid colors work best on camera. Avoid busy patterns or bright whites. Iron your shirt. Yes, even for a video call.

4. Prepare Your Answers to Common Questions

Remote interviews focus on a few key areas:

About Experience

  • "Tell me about yourself."
  • "Walk me through your work history."
  • "What's your biggest professional achievement?"

About Remote Work

  • "Have you worked remotely before?"
  • "How do you stay productive at home?"
  • "How do you handle communication across time zones?"
  • "What remote tools do you use?"

About the Company

  • "Why do you want to work here?"
  • "What do you know about our product?"
  • "Where do you see yourself in 3 years?"

Behavioral Questions

  • "Tell me about a time you solved a difficult problem."
  • "Describe a time you disagreed with a manager."
  • "How do you handle pressure and deadlines?"

Use the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. This works for every behavioral question.

5. Research the Company

Before the interview, know:

  • What the company does
  • Who their customers are
  • Recent news or product launches
  • Their culture and values (from their website and LinkedIn)
  • The interviewer's background (if you know their name, check LinkedIn)

6. Prepare Your Own Questions

Always ask 2-3 questions at the end. It shows you're serious. Good questions:

  • "What does success look like in this role after 90 days?"
  • "How does the team communicate day-to-day?"
  • "What are the biggest challenges for remote workers on this team?"
  • "What's the growth path for this position?"

7. Follow Up Within 24 Hours

Send a thank-you email within 24 hours. Keep it short:

"Hi [Name], thank you for the interview today. I really enjoyed learning about [specific thing]. I'm excited about the possibility of joining the team. Please let me know if you need any additional information. Best, [Your Name]"

8. Common Remote Interview Mistakes

  • Not testing tech beforehand
  • Loud background noise
  • Looking at the screen instead of the camera
  • Multi-tasking (checking phone, typing while talking)
  • Not having questions ready
  • Wearing messy clothes
  • Being late to the meeting link

9. The Day Of

  • Join the call 5 minutes early
  • Have a glass of water nearby
  • Have your CV and the job description in front of you
  • Have a pen and paper for notes
  • Smile and speak clearly
  • Be yourself — but be the professional version
