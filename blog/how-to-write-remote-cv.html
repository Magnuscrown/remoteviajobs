How to Write a Remote Job CV That Gets Noticed

By Magnus Crown • 5 min read • September 2026

Remote jobs attract hundreds of applicants. Your CV has about 6 seconds to impress a recruiter — and it must first pass an AI screener. Here's how to write a CV that wins both.

1. Start With a Strong Summary

Skip the "objective" section. Instead, write a 2-3 sentence professional summary that includes your role, years of experience, and one impressive skill.

Example: "Customer support specialist with 3 years of experience handling 50+ tickets daily via live chat, email, and phone. Fluent in English with proven experience using Zendesk and Salesforce."

2. Use the Right Keywords

ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) scan for keywords. If the job says "customer support" and your CV says "client assistance," you'll be rejected.

Copy the exact keywords from the job description and use them naturally in your CV.

3. Structure Your Experience With Numbers

Vague statements don't work. Use numbers and results.

  • ❌ "Handled customer inquiries."
  • ✅ "Resolved 60+ customer inquiries daily with 95% satisfaction rate."

4. Highlight Remote-Specific Skills

Remote employers want to know: Can you work independently? Can you use remote tools? Add these to your CV:

  • Remote tools: Slack, Zoom, Notion, Trello, Asana, Google Workspace
  • Self-management: "Worked independently across 3 time zones"
  • Communication: "Managed async communication with global team of 12"

5. Keep It Simple for ATS

Design matters for humans — but ATS bots can't read fancy formats. Avoid:

  • Tables and columns
  • Images, logos, and icons
  • Unusual fonts (stick to Arial, Calibri, Inter)
  • Headers and footers (ATS often skips them)

6. Use Standard Section Headings

ATS systems recognize specific headings. Use these exact names:

  • Professional Summary (not "About Me")
  • Work Experience (not "Career History")
  • Education
  • Skills
  • Certifications

7. Include a Skills Section

List 8-12 skills as bullet points. Include both hard skills (software, tools) and soft skills (communication, leadership).

8. Save as PDF (Not Word)

PDFs preserve your formatting. Word files get messed up when opened on different systems.

Name your file properly: FirstName_LastName_CV.pdf — not "resume_final_v3.pdf".

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Typos and grammar errors
  • More than 2 pages (1 page for less than 5 years experience)
  • Unprofessional email (use firstname.lastname@gmail.com)
  • Lying about skills (you will be tested)
