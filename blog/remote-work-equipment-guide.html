Remote Work Equipment Guide for Beginners

By Magnus Crown • 6 min read • September 2026

You don't need $5,000 of equipment to work remotely. But you do need the basics. Here's what to buy at every budget level.

The Absolute Essentials (Under $500)

1. A Reliable Laptop — $300-$500

You don't need a MacBook. For most remote jobs (customer service, data entry, marketing), a basic laptop works:

  • Budget: HP 15, Lenovo IdeaPad — $300-$400
  • Mid-range: Acer Aspire 5, Asus VivoBook — $400-$600
  • Premium: MacBook Air M1 — $700+ (worth it if you can afford it)

Minimum specs: 8GB RAM, 256GB SSD, i3 processor or equivalent

2. Headphones With a Mic — $30-$100

Essential for calls and video meetings. Avoid using laptop speakers/mic.

  • Budget: Sony MDR-ZX110 (wired) — $20
  • Mid: Logitech H390, Jabra Evolve 20 — $40-$60
  • Premium: Sony WH-1000XM5, AirPods Pro — $200-$300

3. Reliable Internet — $20-$50/month

Your career depends on this. Have a backup:

  • Primary Wi-Fi at home
  • Mobile data (MTN, Vodafone, Airtel) as backup
  • Consider a hotspot device for outages

The Important Extras ($500-$1,500)

4. External Monitor — $100-$300

Doubles your productivity. A 24" or 27" monitor changes everything.

  • Budget: HP V24i, Dell P2419H — $100-$150
  • Mid: LG 27", Samsung 27" — $200-$300

5. External Keyboard + Mouse — $30-$100

Laptop keyboards wear out your wrists. Get a proper setup:

  • Keyboard: Logitech K380, Keychron K2 — $40-$100
  • Mouse: Logitech M330, MX Master 3 — $25-$100

6. Webcam — $50-$150

If your laptop's camera is bad, get an external one:

  • Budget: Logitech C270 — $40
  • Mid: Logitech C920 — $70
  • Premium: Logitech Brio 4K — $150

7. Desk + Chair — $200-$1,000

Your back will thank you. Don't work from bed or couch long-term.

  • Budget: Simple desk ($100) + basic chair ($80)
  • Mid: Standing desk ($200) + ergonomic chair ($200)
  • Premium: Herman Miller Sayl chair ($500+)

The Nice-to-Haves ($1,500+)

8. Ring Light — $30-$100

Makes you look professional on video calls. Especially useful for dark rooms.

9. Noise-Cancelling Headphones — $200-$400

If you work in noisy environments. Sony WH-1000XM5 or Bose QuietComfort.

10. UPS (Battery Backup) — $80-$150

Essential in areas with power cuts. Keeps your internet and laptop running during outages.

11. Secondary Laptop — $300+

As backup. Your primary laptop WILL fail eventually. Having a second one saves your career.

What Remote Employers Provide

Many remote companies (especially international ones) provide:

  • Laptop (Apple or high-end Windows)
  • Monitor
  • Headset
  • Internet stipend ($30-$100/month)
  • Home office stipend ($500-$1,000 one-time)

Ask about this during interviews. It's standard at good companies.

Budget Breakdown by Job Type

Customer Service / Data Entry ($200-$500)

  • Basic laptop: $300
  • Headphones with mic: $40
  • Reliable internet: $30/month

Marketing / Content ($500-$1,000)

  • Mid-range laptop: $500
  • External monitor: $150
  • Keyboard + mouse: $80

Developer / Designer ($1,000-$2,000)

  • MacBook or high-end Windows: $1,000+
  • External monitor: $300
  • Ergonomic setup: $400

Where to Buy (Ghana/Africa)

  • Local: Franko Trading, Compughana, Telefonika
  • Jumia — Good deals, delivery available
  • Amazon — Ships to Ghana (use shipping forwarders like Shoptop or Buynow)
  • Aliexpress — Cheap, slow shipping
  • Local Facebook Groups — "Ghana Tech Market" for used deals

Money-Saving Tips

  • Buy used (Facebook Marketplace, OLX) — save 50%+
  • Wait for Black Friday or Amazon Prime Day
  • Start with the cheapest setup that works
  • Upgrade piece by piece as you earn more
  • Ask if the employer provides equipment before you buy

The Bottom Line

You can start working remotely with just a laptop, headphones, and good internet — under $400 total. Don't let equipment stop you from starting. Upgrade as you grow.
