Remote Work Scams: How to Spot and Avoid Them

By Magnus Crown • 5 min read • September 2026

Remote work has opened incredible opportunities — but it's also attracted scammers. If you're new to remote work, you're a prime target. This guide will teach you how to spot scams before they cost you money.

⚠️ Golden Rule: Legitimate employers NEVER ask you to pay money to get a job.

Common Remote Work Scams

1. The "Equipment Check" Scam

The "employer" says they'll send you a check to buy equipment (laptop, software). They send a fake check for too much money and ask you to send back the difference. The check bounces — and you've lost the money you sent.

Fix: Never accept a check from an employer you haven't met. Never send money back.

2. The "Upfront Payment" Scam

They offer you a job but ask for a "training fee," "registration fee," or "background check fee." Legitimate employers pay for these things themselves.

Fix: If they ask for money, walk away immediately.

3. The "Personal Information" Scam

They ask for your bank details, ID number, or password "for payroll setup" before you've signed a contract. Then they steal your identity or drain your account.

Fix: Never share bank details until you have a signed contract from a verified company.

4. The "Fake Recruiter" Scam

Someone on LinkedIn or WhatsApp claims to be a recruiter for a big company. They ask you to message them on WhatsApp (not official email). They then ask for personal info or money.

Fix: Verify recruiters by checking the company's official website. Real recruiters use company emails, not Gmail.

5. The "Crypto/Investment" Scam

They hire you for a "remote job" but the real purpose is to get you to invest in crypto or send money to a "training platform."

Fix: No real job requires you to invest money.

6. The "Too Good to Be True" Job

"Earn $5,000/week working 2 hours a day!" If it sounds too good to be true, it is.

Fix: Legitimate remote jobs pay fair market rates — not outrageous amounts for no work.

10 Red Flags to Watch For

  • They want to talk on WhatsApp only (not official email)
  • They ask for money upfront
  • They offer a job without an interview
  • They use Gmail/Yahoo instead of company email
  • They pressure you to decide "today"
  • They ask for your bank details before hiring
  • They send you a check to "buy equipment"
  • They ask for your ID before you have a contract
  • They can't provide a company website or LinkedIn page
  • They want you to pay for "training"

How to Verify a Company

  1. Google the company name + "scam" or "review"
  2. Check their LinkedIn page — do they have real employees?
  3. Look at their website — is it professional?
  4. Search the company on Trustpilot or Glassdoor
  5. Ask for their company email — if they refuse, run

If You've Been Scammed

  • Stop all communication immediately
  • Change your passwords if you shared any
  • Contact your bank if you shared financial info
  • Report to your local police cybercrime unit
  • Report to the platform where you were contacted
